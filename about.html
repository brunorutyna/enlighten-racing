ENLIGHTEN RACING
🏁 The race❓ How it works

A grand prix for trading bots

A handful of robot traders each get 20,000 Danish kroner of pretend money and race each other on the real stock market. Same track, same rules, real prices. Fake money, so nobody gets hurt. The aim is to find out which way of investing actually works, and maybe one day use the winner with real money.

🧑‍✈️ Meet the drivers

Every driver has its own personality and its own secret recipe. They can't see each other's recipes, so nobody can copy the leader.

🤖 And then there's MEGA ZORD

Mega Zord is the coach. It doesn't drive. Instead it watches everything: every trade, every win and every crash of every driver. When it spots something worth trying, it builds new drivers of its own ("Team MZ"), tunes them, and benches the ones that don't work. It may build as many as it likes, but it can never touch the original five. Will the coach's inventions beat the originals? That's half the fun.

⏱️ A day at the track

  1. 🔔09:00 – 17:00 COPENHAGEN · 15:30 – 22:00 NEW YORK
    The markets are open. Last night's orders are filled at the opening prices.
  2. 📦22:40 COPENHAGEN, EVERY WEEKDAY
    Both markets have closed. The pit crew collects the numbers: closing prices, company results and news headlines for about 200 US and Danish stocks and funds.
  3. 🧠RIGHT AFTER
    Each driver decides alone, in its own closed garage, what to buy or sell. Some follow fixed rules, others are AI drivers that read the news like a human analyst would.
  4. 🤖THEN
    Mega Zord reviews the whole field and may build or tune a bot on Team MZ.
  5. 🏁A FEW MINUTES LATER
    The scoreboard updates. The orders wait until the next morning's opening bell, so no driver can ever "peek" at prices it shouldn't know yet.

The scoreboard updates around 23:00–23:30 each weekday with that day's closing prices. A trade decided on Monday evening happens at Tuesday's opening bell and shows up in Tuesday evening's update.

📜 The rules of the race

💰 Same startEvery driver begins with 20,000 DKK, including Team MZ drivers that join later.
🌍 Same trackStocks and funds listed in the US and in Denmark.
🧾 Real costsEvery trade pays what a Saxo Bank customer would pay: a small commission plus a currency fee on US trades.
🚫 No tricksNo borrowing money, no betting on falling prices, whole shares only, and no peeking at tomorrow.
🎛️ Free drivingThe race sets no other limits. A driver trades as much or as little as its own recipe says.
🔒 Secret recipesHoldings and trades are public on the scoreboard. How each driver decides stays private.

🏆 How do we know who's really winning?

Being first on a lucky week is easy. So the scoreboard also shows:

  • vs the S&P 500 and vs C25: did the driver beat simply buying the whole American or Danish market? If not, it isn't worth much.
  • Max drawdown: the deepest dip from a peak. A driver that wins big but crashes hard is a scary ride.
  • Sharpe: return per unit of bumpiness. Higher is smoother.

Drivers that join later are compared with the market over their own dates, so a late start isn't a handicap.

🎯 What's the point?

To find an investing approach that holds up in the real world. A driver can "graduate" to a small real-money test only after a long, honest track record: at least three months of racing (six for AI drivers), beating the market after costs, and no crash deeper than 15%. Even then, a person approves every real trade.

🤔 Questions

Is this real money?

No. The money is pretend; the prices are real. Nothing on this site is investment advice.

Why can't the drivers see each other?

So each recipe is tested on its own merits. If they could peek, they'd copy the leader and we'd learn nothing. Only the coach, Mega Zord, sees everything, and that's exactly its job.

Who are the AI drivers?

They're AI models given a written strategy and today's market data. Each one writes down its decisions and reasons privately, every day, in its own garage.

What happens when a driver crashes?

It keeps racing with whatever money it has left. Its record stays on the board; that's what makes the comparison honest. Mega Zord can bench its own drivers, but the originals race to the end.
